There is a version of the next decade where a large number of people wake up and find that the thing they did for a living is being done by something else, faster and cheaper, and that "learn to do the next thing" has a shorter and shorter shelf life each time it is said.

I do not think that version is guaranteed. I do think it is likely enough that it is worth building against.

## The part people miss

Most of the conversation about automation is about income, and income matters. But the second loss is quieter and, I think, harder to fix. Work is the thing most people organise their week around. It is where the structure comes from — somewhere to be, something to get better at, other people who notice whether you showed up.

Take the job away and you have to replace two things, not one.

## What I am actually trying to do

Purpose Apps is a small attempt at both:

- **Get money to people.** Build products where real money reaches the people who make them worth using, instead of stopping at whoever owns the platform.
- **Give people something to be committed to.** Structure and progression, not another feed. Something you can be bad at, get better at, and be held to by other people.

The best products do both at once. That is the bar.

## Where it starts

[Provoco](https://provoco.net) is the first one. It is a platform for pickup sport — games, venues, crews, leagues, brackets, live scoring, the whole season rather than a group chat that dies in March.

Sport already does the second half of the job. Millions of people organise their week around a game they are not paid for. What has never worked is the first half: the money stops at the very top, and everyone below the professional tier plays for free.

The long-term ambition for Provoco is to change where that money stops. Not by pretending every player is a pro, but by building the infrastructure that lets value flow down to the levels where almost all of the actual playing happens.

It is a hard problem and I am early. But it is the right kind of hard.
